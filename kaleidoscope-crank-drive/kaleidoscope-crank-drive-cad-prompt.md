# CAD Prompt: Kaleidoscope Crank Drive

Design a complete hand-cranked mechanical drive assembly for a large
stationary kaleidoscope approximately 60 inches (1524 mm) long.

## Function

A person stands at the viewing end of the kaleidoscope and looks through
an eyepiece while turning a hand crank located beside the eyepiece.

The crank must rotate a large circular object chamber located at the
opposite end of the kaleidoscope, approximately 60 inches away.

The kaleidoscope body itself remains stationary. Only the circular front
object chamber rotates.

## Drive System

Use a long steel drive shaft running parallel to the kaleidoscope body
from the viewing end to the front end.

The hand crank connects directly to this drive shaft.

Support the long drive shaft with multiple mounted bearings along its
length to prevent sagging, vibration, and misalignment.

At the far end of the drive shaft, mount a small roller-chain sprocket.

Use #40 ANSI roller chain to connect this small sprocket to a larger
sprocket mounted on the axle of the rotating object chamber.

Design for approximately a 5:1 reduction ratio so that approximately
five rotations of the hand crank produce one complete rotation of the
object chamber.

Use approximately:

-   12-tooth #40 sprocket on the drive shaft
-   60-tooth #40 sprocket on the object-chamber axle

## Front Object Chamber

Assume a circular rotating chamber approximately 30 inches (762 mm) in
diameter.

The chamber should rotate on a central steel axle supported
independently by two mounted bearings.

The large 60-tooth sprocket mounts securely to this axle.

Do not support the weight of the rotating chamber with the chain or
crank drive. Its axle and bearings must carry the structural load.

## Hand Crank

Place the crank at the viewing end, beside and slightly below the
eyepiece so that a standing adult can look through the kaleidoscope
while comfortably turning the crank with one hand.

Use a crank arm approximately 7 inches (178 mm) from shaft centre to
handle centre.

Include a freely rotating cylindrical hand grip approximately 4 to 5
inches long.

The crank must mechanically attach to the long drive shaft.

## Long Drive Shaft

Start with a nominal 3/4-inch steel shaft for the CAD concept, but make
shaft diameter parametrically adjustable.

Support it with bearing blocks at the viewing end, near the front drive
mechanism, and at least one intermediate location.

All bearings must be coaxially aligned.

## Chain Tensioning

Make the small-sprocket/drive-shaft mounting position adjustable using
slotted mounting holes or another simple mechanical tensioning system.

The chain should be removable and replaceable.

Include reasonable clearance around the chain and sprockets for a future
protective guard.

## CAD Components to Create

Create each as a separate component or assembly:

1.  Hand crank arm
2.  Rotating hand grip and its pin/axle
3.  Long drive shaft
4.  Drive-shaft bearing mounts
5.  Intermediate drive-shaft bearing mount
6.  12-tooth #40 drive sprocket
7.  60-tooth #40 driven sprocket
8.  #40 roller-chain path
9.  Adjustable chain-tensioning mount
10. Front object-chamber axle
11. Two front-axle bearing mounts
12. Hub/flange for attaching the rotating chamber to its axle
13. Structural mounting plates/brackets for attaching the mechanism to a
    wooden kaleidoscope frame
14. Removable protective chain/sprocket guard

## Assembly Requirements

Show all components assembled in their correct mechanical relationship.

The hand crank and long drive shaft must rotate together.

The small sprocket must rotate with the long drive shaft.

The chain transfers rotation to the large sprocket.

The large sprocket and front axle rotate together.

The front object chamber attaches to the front axle and therefore
rotates at approximately one-fifth the crank speed.

The kaleidoscope body and support structure remain stationary.

Use standard off-the-shelf bearings, shaft collars, sprockets, chain,
fasteners, keys, hubs, and steel shafting wherever possible rather than
designing custom versions of commercially available hardware.

Make custom CAD parts primarily for the crank, mounting plates,
brackets, chamber hub, guards, and structural interfaces.

## Parametric Design

Make these dimensions editable parameters:

-   kaleidoscope length
-   object chamber diameter
-   drive shaft diameter
-   front axle diameter
-   crank arm length
-   bearing spacing
-   sprocket tooth counts
-   sprocket centre distance
-   mounting plate thickness
-   chain clearance

Do not assume that the preliminary shaft diameters are structurally
adequate for the final build. The final front axle, drive shaft,
bearings, hubs, fasteners, and frame must be sized after the actual
rotating chamber weight and loading are known.

## Output

Produce an exploded assembly view, assembled view, and individual
manufacturable components.

Clearly identify which parts should be fabricated and which should be
purchased as standard mechanical components.

Include mounting holes and realistic mechanical clearances.

Avoid decorative features. Prioritize a simple, robust mechanism that
can be fabricated in a small workshop and assembled using commonly
available hardware.
